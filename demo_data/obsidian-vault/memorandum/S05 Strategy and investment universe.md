Aurora seeks differences between market prices and the fundamental value of European companies. The typical investment horizon is 18–36 months for long positions and 3–18 months for shorts. The target annual net return of 6–9% and volatility of 6–10% are multi-year objectives, not promises or loss limits.

## Return drivers

The long portfolio favours companies with sustainable returns on capital, cash generation and valuations that do not reflect their growth potential. The team seeks an indicative 25% margin of safety relative to estimated value and an identifiable catalyst: margin improvement, debt reduction, corporate restructuring or capital reallocation.

The short portfolio targets companies with structural deterioration, weak earnings-to-cash conversion, excessive leverage or expectations that are difficult to meet. Every short thesis must address the cost and availability of stock borrowing, recall risk and a sharp price rally scenario. A high valuation alone does not justify a short position.

## Universe and instruments

The research universe comprises approximately 700 companies listed in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland. The ordinary minimum market capitalisation is EUR 750 million, with at least EUR 5 million of average daily traded value over 60 sessions. Up to 10% of NAV may be invested in smaller companies provided they meet portfolio liquidity requirements.

| Component | Scenario range or purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Long equities | 70–110% of NAV; normally 25–40 issuers |
| Direct equity shorts | 10–40% of NAV; normally 10–20 issuers |
| Short index futures | 0–30% of NAV; market risk hedging |
| Net exposure | 20–70% of NAV, including equity futures |
| Gross equity exposure | Maximum 160% of NAV |
| Currencies | Forward hedging; maximum 15% aggregate residual currency exposure |
| Cash | EUR and other operational currencies; no structured or unlisted investments |

## Sell discipline and thesis review

A position is reduced when the price reaches estimated value, a better opportunity becomes available or the facts supporting the thesis change. A 15% loss from the entry price triggers a formal review rather than an automatic sale. For shorts, a 10% increase in the share price triggers review; risk is reduced immediately if the stock borrow is no longer reliable.
